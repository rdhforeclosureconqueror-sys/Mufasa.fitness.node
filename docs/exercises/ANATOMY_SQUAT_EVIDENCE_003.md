# Squat evidence package 003 — research draft
E9 Gullett et al. 2009 https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19002072/ — 15 trained adults; front and back squat showed similar measured EMG, back squat higher knee compression and extensor moments in tested conditions. Relative load comparison, not universal ranking.
E10 Bryanton et al. 2012 https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22797000/ — ten trained women, 50–90% 1RM; depth increased knee extensor relative effort, depth and load increased hip extensor effort. Joint-level estimates not individual muscle force.
E11 https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10987311/ — biomechanical review; trunk relative to tibia and anterior load placement modify hip/knee demands. Review not direct goblet EMG.
E12 https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20508473/ — 28 participants, unloaded and additional 50% bodyweight squat kinematics; not EMG.

## Review queue
- bodyweight_squat: bilateral unloaded, record depth and stance. Quadriceps and gluteus maximus candidate knee/hip extensors; spinal erector and abdominal stabilization hypotheses. E12 direct kinematics only; E10 indirect loaded evidence.
- goblet_squat: anterior handheld load; E11 supports posture-dependent joint demands. Quadriceps and gluteus maximus candidates, trunk and upper limb load holding hypotheses. Direct individual muscle evidence needed.
- back_squat: bar on upper back; distinguish high/low bar and depth. E9/E10 support knee and hip extensor demand; rectus femoris, vasti, gluteus maximus candidates; erector spinae, hamstrings and plantar flexors require role review. 
- front_squat: front rack; E9/E11 support technique-dependent joint demands; quadriceps and gluteus maximus candidates; trunk and front rack support hypotheses. Do not infer greater quadriceps activation universally from front loading.

Geometry backlog: vastus intermedius (deep), adductor magnus differentiation, deeper trunk musculature. No clinical approval or active mappings. Sources inspected at abstract/excerpt level; full-text review and direct goblet/bodyweight muscle evidence pending.
