# Course Corrections, Scientific Cautions and Guardrails

These corrections were deliberately preserved during the course review.

- Do not equate screening with diagnosis.
- Do not infer muscle activation from camera movement.
- Dynamic knee collapse inward is dynamic knee valgus, not varus.
- Contributors to dynamic valgus can include hip strength/control, ankle dorsiflexion, trunk/pelvis, technique, fatigue and anatomy; avoid single-cause claims.
- Stretching alone does not solve a strength/control deficit.
- Tuck Jump Assessment is not a standalone ACL-injury predictor.
- Course ROM/norm values are context, not universal pass/fail cutoffs.
- Match/training surveillance supports risk reduction, not deterministic injury prediction.
- Late-game injury clustering is a prompt to investigate, not proof of cause.
- Female players should not automatically receive lighter loads.
- RED-S can affect all sexes and should not be diagnosed by the coaching engine.
- Hz alone does not determine GPS accuracy.
- Raw high-g accelerometer events are not automatically rugby collisions.
- VBT velocity zones are not universal.
- Mean, mean-propulsive and peak velocity are different.
- Weightlifting derivatives are valid endpoints; full lifts are optional.
- Triple-extension similarity does not guarantee transfer.
- Do not encode the course clean cue to deliberately shift bodyweight onto the forefoot from the floor.
- “Jump/catch” can be a teaching cue, but evaluate bar path, extension and balance.
- Shorter rest is not inherently superior for hypertrophy.
- Exercise number is not a proxy for session quality.
- Strongman awkwardness does not automatically create greater transfer.
- Energy systems overlap; do not teach clean switching.
- Do not reduce fatigue to “lactic acid.”
- RSA is a subset of broader rugby RHIE.
- >100% MAS means speed above MAS, not oxygen uptake above VO2max.
- Course “Tabetha” terminology was treated as Tabata.
- A 60 m average speed is not true MSS.
- 76–94% sprint-speed running can still have useful roles.
- “CNS fatigue” should not be quantified as a precise software score without a valid measurement.
- Closed COD tests are not the same as open agility.
- Position is a modifier, not a stereotype.
- Same-protocol longitudinal comparison + measurement confidence should govern interpretation.
- Medical/governing-body decisions override automated training logic.
