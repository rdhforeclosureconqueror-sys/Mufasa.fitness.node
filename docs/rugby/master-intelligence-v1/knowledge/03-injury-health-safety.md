# Injury, Health, Safety and Availability

## Injury surveillance
Rugby injury: a physical complaint arising from match or training. Store medical-attention and time-loss definitions separately.

Incidence:
injuries / exposure hours × 1000

Separate training exposure from match exposure. Use actual participation/minutes where practical.

## Severity
Course bands preserved:
- slight: 0–1 days
- minimal: 2–3 days
- mild: 4–7 days
- moderate: 8–28 days
- severe: >28 days

Burden is commonly days lost per 1000 player-hours.

## Mechanism fields
Store meaningful mechanisms such as:
tackle, tackled, ruck, maul, scrum, run, change of direction, collision, landing.

Preserve tackler versus ball-carrier role where relevant.

## Interpretation
- Track individual and team trends.
- Track burden, mechanism, position and recurrence.
- Availability is a meaningful performance outcome.
- Surveillance supports risk reduction; it does not produce deterministic injury prediction.
- Previous injury is relevant context, not proof of future cause.
- Acute and gradual-onset problems should be distinguished.
- Late-game injury clustering can prompt investigation but should not be declared causal without evidence.
- Match injury incidence is generally higher than training incidence; benchmark context matters.

## Warm-up / prevention
Multicomponent Activate-style warm-ups can be useful. Compliance and implementation matter.

## Concussion
Recognize · Remove · Refer.
Do not automate diagnosis or return-to-play clearance. Follow governing-body and qualified-medical protocols.

## Other hard gates
Pain, neurological symptoms, cervical red flags, suspected concussion, and medical-hold status override automated physical progression.

## Availability states
Useful coach-facing states include:
- Game eligible
- Limited
- Medical hold
The detailed medical reason can remain role-restricted.

## Safety hierarchy
Safety and authorized medical decisions override performance optimization. Performance staff should receive the minimum actionable state necessary.
