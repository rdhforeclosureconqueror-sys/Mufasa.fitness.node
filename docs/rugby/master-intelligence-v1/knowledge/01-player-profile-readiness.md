# Player Profile, Lifestyle and Readiness

## Player profile dimensions
Track body size, body composition, strength, power, speed, conditioning and lifestyle/readiness separately.

### Body size and composition
- Functional mass appropriate to role is the goal; bigger is not automatically better.
- Interpret mass with body composition, strength, power, speed and conditioning.
- Avoid one male/female body-composition target.
- Track method and protocol when body composition is measured.

### Strength and power
- Absolute and relative strength both matter.
- Power is distinct from strength and reflects force expressed at velocity.
- Maintain longitudinal context rather than one-off rankings.

### Speed
- Acceleration and maximal sprint speed are separate.
- Preserve split distances, start type, surface, device and timing method.
- MSS capability is separate from actual match/training exposure.

### Conditioning
Keep at least:
- aerobic fitness / MAS or other aerobic metric
- repeated-sprint ability
- repeated high-intensity effort ability
- peak output versus repeatability / decrement

## Daily/weekly readiness
Useful inputs:
- sleep quantity
- sleep quality
- fatigue
- soreness
- life stress
- mental fatigue
- mood
- fueling
- hydration
- recent match minutes
- running load
- contact/collision exposure

Use trends and context. Readiness is not a diagnosis and one poor score does not automatically cancel the training plan.

## Social/cognitive context
Any social-media or device-use information is optional cognitive-load context, not surveillance.

## Nutrition
- Carbohydrate and protein: 4 kcal/g.
- Fat: 9 kcal/g.
- Carbohydrate availability can be periodized to training demand.
- Use individualized food-first practice.
- Do not hard-code a 45-minute anabolic window.
- Supplement advice requires contamination and anti-doping safeguards.

## Female athlete / RED-S context
- Use the same core training principles while incorporating relevant sex-specific health context.
- Do not prescribe lighter training solely because the athlete is female.
- Population averages are context, not destiny; individual monitoring overrides them.
- Triad: low energy availability ± disordered eating + menstrual dysfunction + low bone mineral density.
- RED-S is broader and can affect athletes of any sex.
- Energy availability is conceptual: (energy intake − exercise energy expenditure) / fat-free mass. Avoid false precision from casual logs.
- Pattern flags can include body-mass change, fueling concern, high load, menstrual change, fatigue/performance decline and bone-stress history.
- Hormonal contraception complicates bleeding-pattern interpretation.
- Coach role: support, notice patterns, refer. Do not diagnose.
- Treat sensitive health information as privacy-restricted.
