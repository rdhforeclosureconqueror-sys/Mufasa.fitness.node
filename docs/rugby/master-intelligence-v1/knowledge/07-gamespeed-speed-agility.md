# Gamespeed, Speed and Agility

## Gamespeed model
Gamespeed = physical speed + movement skill + perception + decision + rugby-task execution.

Distinct qualities:
- linear speed
- pre-planned COD
- reactive agility
- Gamespeed

Movement functions:
- transition: prepare, scan, reposition
- initiation: first movement after decision
- actualisation: acceleration / curved / linear expression

## Closed vs open
Correct terminology here is closed skill vs open skill.
Progression:
fundamental movement → closed skill → combinations → simple reactive → multi-choice reactive → rugby-specific open drill → small-sided game.

Closed-test success is not proof of game agility.
If open performance is poor, decompose:
physical movement / perception / decision / rugby skill.

## Linear speed
Separate acceleration and MSS.
Position can influence emphasis, but individual profile overrides.
Direct sprint exposure is necessary; strength and plyometrics support it but do not replace it.

Acceleration methods:
- free sprint
- resisted sprint
- hills
- horizontal plyometrics
- strength/power support

MSS methods:
- flying sprints
- free high-velocity sprints
- measured exposure >90% and >95% MSS when reliable

Heavy resistance does not replace high-velocity exposure.
Assisted/overspeed sprinting is optional and advanced.

## Acute variables
Course guides:
- acceleration often 5–30 m
- MSS work often 30–60 m total build-up/sprint context
- roughly 3–8 reps and 1–2 sets
- rough recovery start: ~1 min per 10 m, then individualize

Track:
best time/velocity, average, decrement, total sprint distance, exposure above individualized thresholds.

Do not quantify “CNS fatigue” as a precise score without a valid measure.
76–94% sprint-speed running is not automatically useless; it can have technical, rehab, tempo or speed-endurance roles.

## Annual planning
Use functional phases:
transition/off-season → early preseason mechanics → later preseason free accel/MSS/reactive specificity → in-season lower-volume quality → targeted top-ups in breaks.

Do not assume match play gives every player adequate MSS exposure.

## Agility
Course progression preserved:
closed mechanics → reactive/open → game-specific scenario.

Examples:
- side shuffle
- cut
- mirror
- Run to Daylight
- rugby-specific scenario / SSG

Recovery depends on purpose: quality versus agility under fatigue.
Small-sided-game load depends on pitch size, player numbers, rules, contact, duration and work-rest.
Meaningful opponent/teammate information is often more representative than arbitrary lights or cones.
Monitor both movement execution and decision outcome.
