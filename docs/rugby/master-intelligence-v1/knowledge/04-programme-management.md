# Programme Management and Periodisation

## Planning hierarchy
SEASON / PHASE → MESOCYCLE → MICROCYCLE → SESSION → EXERCISE / DRILL

A microcycle is often a week but should remain flexible. A mesocycle can commonly span about 2–8 weeks.

Store planned and actual work separately.

## Team + position + individual
PROGRAMME = TEAM TEMPLATE + POSITION-GROUP MODIFIERS + INDIVIDUAL MODIFIERS

Individual modifiers include:
- current profile
- training age
- readiness
- injury/availability
- match minutes
- running/contact load
- technical competency
- equipment / coach capability

## Periodisation models
### Linear
Sequential emphasis. Often beginner-friendly. Emphasis does not mean complete exclusion of other important qualities.

### Undulating / DUP / high-low
Frequent variation of quality or load. Quality undulation and load undulation are different. Match day anchors the microcycle. Readiness can modify dose without erasing the session objective.

### Block
Accumulation → transmutation → realization is a useful teaching structure. Residuals are qualitative and athlete-specific; do not hard-code exact decay dates. Hybrid models are often sensible in rugby.

## Hypertrophy
Goal: functional mass that supports rugby.
Course guides:
- ~6–15 reps
- ~10–20 hard sets per muscle group per week
- ~60–85% 1RM
- often ~RPE 7–9 / reasonably close to failure
Keep RPE and RIR as separate fields.
Use compound patterns, purposeful variation, progression and deloads.
Condition concurrently as needed.
Monitor mass/composition together with strength, power, speed and conditioning.

## Strength and power
Strength is a force foundation; power is force expressed quickly.
Course example: ~5 weeks strength emphasis then ~3 weeks power emphasis, while maintaining the non-emphasized quality.
Power work should be quality-first: jumps, loaded jumps, medicine balls, Olympic derivatives, ballistic throws, sleds.
Velocity/output drop can trigger rest extension, rep reduction or set termination.

## In-season
Continue strength and power.
Common practical default: ~2 exposures/week, adaptable.
Reduce unnecessary volume before removing meaningful intensity/quality.
Use MD-relative scheduling.
Match minutes, running load, contact load, travel, readiness and congestion modify the dose.
Micro-dosing is valid when schedule constraints are high.

## Large squads
Do not create 30 unrelated programmes. Use common movement families with progression/regression and grouping by:
- goal
- training age
- position demand
- availability
An athlete can belong to multiple groups at once.
