# Athlete #001 — Preseason Functional-Mass + Speed Pilot

Status: de-identified case-study resource, not a medical record  
Date: 2026-11-24

## 1. Athlete-defined objective

USER-REPORTED:
- current body mass: approximately **140 lb**;
- desired long-term body mass: **160 lb**;
- time window discussed: approximately **2.5 months / 10–11 weeks**;
- current self-estimated speed: approximately **16–17 mph** while feeling tight;
- desired speed capability: maintain/achieve **18–19 mph**, with an aspirational **20 mph** ceiling;
- desired repeatability: reproduce high sprint outputs across multiple efforts with controlled recovery and stop when meaningful speed degradation begins.

The athlete's actual success criterion is NOT simply "weigh 160."

Preferred success definition:
> Add useful mass while preserving or improving relative power, acceleration, maximum velocity, repeatability and movement quality.

## 2. Training background relevant to programming

USER-REPORTED:
- historically played rugby around 155–160 lb and reported very low body fat at that time;
- historically used little heavy barbell resistance training;
- now wants to introduce heavier resistance training;
- does not prefer weighted barbell squats;
- is interested in power cleans;
- has access to a curved treadmill and expects to perform much early speed work indoors;
- field-specific running should increase closer to competition.

Programming implication:
Treat heavy resistance and Olympic-lift loading as a competency/tolerance progression, not as if the athlete already has years of technical barbell training.

## 3. Historical load-response warning

USER-REPORTED:
During a prior 7s speed push, sprint velocity increased quickly (roughly 15 to 17 mph over a short period) but knee swelling appeared afterward.

Do NOT encode a diagnosis from this statement.

Performance-system implication:
- avoid another zero-to-max sprint-load jump;
- monitor 24-hour joint/tissue response;
- if swelling/pain recurs or persists, stop normal progression and route to appropriate health evaluation according to the Rugby Coaching OS safety boundary.

Keep this case de-identified in public documentation.

## 4. Body-mass target interpretation

THREAD-CONFIRMED:
140 → 160 in ~10–11 weeks is an aggressive scale-weight target if the expectation is that nearly all 20 lb will be new contractile muscle.

Rugby HQ should treat 160 as a destination, not force weekly scale gain at the expense of:
- speed;
- relative power;
- movement quality;
- joint/tendon response;
- unnecessary fat gain.

A better decision loop is:
- gain;
- assess;
- confirm speed/power/movement response;
- continue or adjust.

## 5. Proposed 10-week operating model

This is the case-study plan discussed in-thread. It is a working model, not an official governing-body prescription.

### Block 0 — Foundation & baseline
Approx. 1 week.

Objectives:
- establish movement and readiness baseline;
- establish speed/power/conditioning baselines;
- introduce safe heavy-resistance technique;
- establish power-clean progression rather than immediately maximizing load;
- build tissue tolerance;
- record protocol/device/surface.

No need to "prove fitness" in Week 1.

### Block 1 — Functional mass + speed exposure
Approx. Weeks 2–4.

Primary:
- hypertrophy / useful mass.

Secondary:
- max-velocity technique/exposure.

Maintenance:
- aerobic capacity;
- mobility/movement quality;
- rugby skill;
- other qualities at minimum effective dose.

Default session order on combined days:
warm-up → speed exposure → explosive work → hypertrophy/strength → accessory/recovery.

### Block 2 — Functional mass + power
Approx. Weeks 5–7.

Primary:
- hypertrophy remains important because mass gain is still an athlete objective.

Secondary:
- power.

Speed:
- maintenance exposure.

Power-clean derivatives may become more prominent as technical competency improves.

### Block 3 — Speed conversion + repeatability
Approx. Weeks 8–10.

Primary:
- max velocity / acceleration.

Secondary:
- RSA / RHIE.

Hypertrophy and strength:
- maintenance dose.

Increase field-specific work as competition approaches:
- acceleration angles;
- horizontal force;
- upright mechanics;
- deceleration;
- change of direction;
- reactive agility;
- ball/rugby constraints.

The curved treadmill remains a training tool, not a field-speed equivalency test.

## 6. Power clean role

Use the power clean as a **power converter**, not the sole mass-builder.

Potential exercise-skill tree already compatible with the Rugby Coaching OS competency concept:
- movement prerequisites;
- jump shrug;
- clean pull;
- hang power clean;
- power clean.

Coach-verified competency should determine progression.

## 7. Sprint KPI model

Do not track only "number of sprints."

For each repeated high-speed set, capture:
- peak/best speed;
- average speed;
- slowest speed;
- percentage decrement;
- reps above quality threshold;
- recovery interval;
- session stop reason.

Future target example:
- athlete can produce 18–19+ mph;
- repeat several high-quality efforts;
- performance decay remains controlled.

Do not hard-code a universal acceptable decrement until protocol/source is verified.

## 8. Weekly monitoring

Suggested case-study fields:
- body mass;
- body-composition method/result if available;
- peak speed;
- speed exposure > individualized threshold;
- resistance-training volume/load;
- jump/power metric;
- RPE;
- sleep;
- fatigue;
- soreness;
- knee/joint response;
- perceived tightness;
- planned vs actual;
- nutrition adherence/context.

## 9. Block-level decision rule

At each unload/retest point ask:

> Did the athlete become heavier AND better?

Positive pattern:
- mass increases;
- speed stable/improving;
- jump/power stable/improving;
- movement quality acceptable;
- joint response acceptable.

Negative pattern:
- mass increases;
- speed materially declines;
- power declines;
- movement quality worsens;
- recurrent swelling/pain increases.

If negative, stop chasing scale weight and re-prioritize.

## 10. Athlete #001 product purpose

This case should become Rugby HQ's first complete longitudinal performance example:

**Baseline → Goal → Needs Analysis → Block Prescription → Training → Monitoring → Retest → Adaptation → Next Block**

It is valuable because it tests the software and coaching workflow simultaneously.

The public resource should remain de-identified. Real athlete health/sensitive records belong only in permissioned production systems.
