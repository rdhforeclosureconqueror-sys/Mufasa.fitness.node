# Roots & Awareness — Week 1 Instructor Curriculum

**Program:** Milele Fit × Pan-African Connection | **Limb:** Yama | **Status:** curriculum draft pending Akan cultural-source and instructor review.

## Cultural and teaching safeguards
Yama is the first limb in Patañjali's Yoga Sūtras (2.30); Adinkra symbols arise from Akan cultural traditions. The pairings below are modern thematic comparisons, not claims of common origins or identical meanings. Confirm each symbol's visual form, spelling and interpretation with an Akan cultural educator before publication. All stories are original instructional parables, not traditional Akan folktales. Avoid forced reconciliation, trauma disclosures, breath retention or required touch. Brahmacharya traditionally refers to celibacy or sexual restraint; modern moderation readings must be labeled as adaptations. The original Week 1 preview remains separate.

## Weekly live class — 90 minutes
10 min consent, welcome and grounding; 15 min introduce Yama and the five restraints; 15 min introduce Akan Adinkra and the week's pairings with clear cultural distinctions; 10 min read one original story and discuss; 20 min accessible yoga with seated alternatives; 10 min gentle natural breathing; 5 min optional sound bowls or silence; 5 min commitments and closing. Offer opt-outs throughout.

## Day 1: Ahimsa × Bi Nka Bi

**Theme:** Non-harming. **Learning objective:** Explain the yoga concept in your own words, describe the Adinkra symbol's distinct cultural meaning with appropriate sourcing, and choose one observable action.

### Story — The Two Paths
Two neighbors repeatedly block one another's path to the well. A child asks each to move one stone so both can pass. Neither loses their dignity; both gain safe passage.

### Daily facilitation (15–20 minutes)
1. Welcome (2 min): Invite an optional one-word check-in; passing is always welcome.
2. Teach (4 min): Explain Ahimsa and introduce Bi Nka Bi as a distinct Akan teaching lens; do not claim equivalence.
3. Story (3 min): Read the original parable and invite one interpretation.
4. Movement and breath (5–7 min): Supported mountain pose, seated rest, slow natural breathing. Keep breathing naturally; offer chair-based options and no-touch alternatives.
5. Reflection (3 min): Where can I reduce harm without abandoning my boundaries?
6. Commitment (1 min): Invite a specific, voluntary action for the next 24 hours.

**Participant journal:** What did each tradition teach me separately? Where did I see a useful connection? What will I do differently today?

**Instructor assessment:** Participant can name the distinction between the two traditions and identify one action; no personal disclosure is required.
