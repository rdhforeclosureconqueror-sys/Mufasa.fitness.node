# Roots & Awareness — Week 2: Niyama × Adinkra

**Status:** Instructor curriculum draft; cultural-source and yoga-teaching review required before public release.

## Teaching frame
Niyama is the second limb in the Yoga Sūtras (2.32): śauca (purity/cleanliness), santoṣa (contentment), tapas (discipline/austerity), svādhyāya (self-study/recitation), and īśvara-praṇidhāna (devotion/surrender to Ishvara). Interpretations vary by tradition. Adinkra symbols belong to Akan cultural traditions and are used here as **distinct thematic companions**, not historical equivalents. All stories below are original modern instructional parables, not Akan folktales. Verify names, symbol images and interpretations with an Akan educator; do not imply historical shared origins. Do not impose a single theology. Consent, opt-out, no forced touch and unforced natural breathing apply to every practice.

## Instructor-led weekly session (90 minutes)
10 min welcome, consent and optional check-in; 15 min history and five Niyamas; 15 min Adinkra cultural context and distinctions; 10 min original story plus dialogue; 20 min accessible yoga with rest choices; 10 min natural breathing and silent reflection; 5 min optional sound bowls; 5 min journal and take-home commitment. No forced breath holds, required touch, or personal disclosures.

## Day 1 — Saucha × Sesa Wo Suban

**Learning focus:** Cleanliness and purification.

**Akan cultural note:** Sesa Wo Suban is associated with transformation of character; it does not literally mean cleanliness.

### Original teaching story: The Cleared Courtyard
A caretaker sweeps a courtyard each morning, but continues speaking harshly to visitors. A child asks whether the courtyard is truly clean when the welcome remains unkind. The caretaker begins caring for both the space and her conduct.

### Guided daily practice (15–20 minutes)
1. **Arrival (2 min):** Offer a one-word check-in, including the option to pass.
2. **Teach (4 min):** Explain Saucha in the yoga tradition; explain Sesa Wo Suban separately in its Akan context. Ask students to identify a thematic connection without equating them.
3. **Story (3 min):** Read the original parable. Invite an optional interpretation.
4. **Movement and breath (5–7 min):** Gentle joint mobility and comfortable standing or seated posture. Maintain natural breathing and offer seated or rest alternatives.
5. **Reflect (3 min):** What could I clear from my surroundings or habits without judging myself?
6. **Apply (1 min):** Choose one small, observable action for the next 24 hours.

**Participant journal:** What does the yoga teaching ask of me? What does this Adinkra symbol mean in its own tradition? What one action can I practice today?

**Instructor check:** Learner can describe the distinction between traditions and identify a realistic daily action. Do not grade personal disclosure or physical ability.

## Day 2 — Santosha × Akoma

**Learning focus:** Contentment.

**Akan cultural note:** Akoma is commonly associated with patience and tolerance; contentment is a modern thematic comparison.

### Original teaching story: The Bowl That Was Enough
Two potters make bowls. One compares every bowl with another's and cannot enjoy the work. The other notices what is useful and beautiful while still practicing to improve. Both continue learning, but only one allows herself satisfaction along the way.

## Day 3 — Tapas × Wawa Aba

**Learning focus:** Disciplined effort.

**Akan cultural note:** Wawa Aba is associated with toughness, perseverance and resilience.

### Original teaching story: The Seed and the Dry Season
A gardener waters a seed in small measured amounts each morning. Neighbors demand immediate growth. The gardener continues patient work, resting when needed. When rain comes, the roots are ready.

## Day 4 — Svadhyaya × Sankofa

**Learning focus:** Self-study.

**Akan cultural note:** Sankofa evokes learning from the past; it is not a traditional translation of svadhyaya.

### Original teaching story: The Book of Footprints
A traveler studies the marks left on a path and recognizes places where haste caused wrong turns. Rather than walking backward forever, she learns from the route and chooses her next step more carefully.

## Day 5 — Ishvara Pranidhana × Gye Nyame

**Learning focus:** Devotion and surrender.

**Akan cultural note:** Gye Nyame is commonly understood in relation to God's supremacy; distinguish Akan religious context from yoga's Ishvara and welcome non-theistic participation.

### Original teaching story: The River and the Oars
A canoeist rows carefully but cannot command the current. She learns to steer where she can, seek help, and release the illusion of controlling every wave.

## Day 7 — Niyama integration × Nkonsonkonson

**Learning focus:** Commitment and community.

**Akan cultural note:** Nkonsonkonson represents human relations, unity and interdependence.

### Original teaching story: The Seven Links
Seven neighbors agree to maintain a shared garden. Each has different strengths and schedules. When one misses a day, the group adjusts the plan rather than shaming anyone, preserving both responsibility and care.
