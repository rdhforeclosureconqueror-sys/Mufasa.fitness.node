TITLE: NASM-STYLE MOVEMENT SCIENCE – SUMMARY

KINETIC CHAIN
Human movement = integrated system of:
- Nervous system (control)
- Muscular system (prime movers, stabilizers, synergists)
- Skeletal system (levers and joints)

MUSCLE ACTIONS
- CONCENTRIC: muscle shortens, accelerates movement.
- ECCENTRIC: muscle lengthens under tension, decelerates and controls.
- ISOMETRIC: muscle length constant, stabilizes joints.

MUSCLE ROLES
- PRIME MOVER: main driver (e.g. glute max in hip extension).
- SYNERGIST: assists the prime mover.
- STABILIZER: prevents unwanted movement.
- ANTAGONIST: performs opposite action, must coordinate and release.

PLANES AND AXES
- SAGITTAL / medial-lateral axis: flexion/extension.
- FRONTAL / anterior-posterior axis: abduction/adduction, lateral flexion.
- TRANSVERSE / longitudinal axis: rotation, horizontal ab/adduction.

POSTURAL ALIGNMENT CHECKPOINTS (standing)
- Feet: straight, hip-width, weight balanced over tripod of foot.
- Knees: in line with 2nd/3rd toe, not caving inward.
- Hips/pelvis: neutral, not excessively anterior or posterior tilted.
- Lumbar spine: natural curve, not extreme extension.
- Thoracic: tall, no rounding.
- Shoulders: level, not internally rotated or elevated.
- Head: neutral, ears over shoulders, not forward.

COMMON COMPENSATIONS
- FEET: flattening (overpronation), turning out.
- KNEES: valgus (caving in) or varus (bowing out).
- HIPS / LOW BACK: excessive arch or rounding.
- SHOULDERS: elevation, protraction, internal rotation.
- HEAD: forward head.

PROGRAMMING PHASES (inspired by NASM OPT)
- STABILIZATION:
  Goal: joint stability, control, endurance.
  Focus: balance, slow tempo, controlled breathing, light loads.

- STRENGTH:
  Goal: increase force production and coordination under load.
  Focus: 6–12 reps, moderate tempo, multi-joint lifts.

- POWER:
  Goal: rate of force development.
  Focus: explosive intent, jump / throw / sprint patterns, couple strength + speed drills.

KEY PRINCIPLES
- Use integrated warm-ups: tissue prep → mobility → activation → movement prep.
- Train movements before muscles; planes before body parts.
- Quality reps first, then quantity.
