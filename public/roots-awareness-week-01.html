The Art of Living in Harmony

Explore Yama, the first limb of yoga, alongside Mpatapo, an Akan Adinkra symbol of reconciliation and peacemaking. These are distinct traditions in conversation, not historically identical teachings.

1 · Yama: five ethical restraints

In Yoga Sutra 2.30: Ahimsa (non-harming), Satya (truthfulness), Asteya (non-stealing), Brahmacharya (traditionally celibacy or sexual restraint; sometimes broadened today to moderation), and Aparigraha (non-grasping).

Consider: how do our actions shape relationships?

2 · Mpatapo: the knot of reconciliation

Mpatapo is associated with reconciliation, peacemaking, and restored harmony in Akan Adinkra symbolism. Reconciliation need not mean forgetting harm, forcing forgiveness, or surrendering boundaries.

Consider: what does repair require after conflict?

3 · Teaching story: The Village and the Knot

Original instructional parable; not a traditional Akan folktale.

Two neighboring families share a village but fall into conflict over land. Accusations grow, and even their children stop playing together. An elder gathers them beneath a tree and offers each person a strand of rope. Each strand moves independently. She ties them into a knot: each strand keeps its identity, yet its movement now affects the others.

When one family asks whether peace requires forgetting harm, she replies: “Peace without truth is only silence.” To the other she says: “You cannot demand reconciliation while continuing to cause harm.” The families begin to listen. Nothing is solved immediately, but they begin the work of repair.

Memory cues: Village → Conflict → Elder → Knot → Repair.

Connect the five yamas

Ahimsa: stop harm. Satya: speak truth. Asteya: respect what belongs to others. Brahmacharya: practice restraint. Aparigraha: examine possessiveness. Mpatapo gives a distinct Akan image of reconciliation and peacemaking.

4 · Guided class practice (90 minutes)

  1. 10 min welcome and grounding
  2. 15 min Yama and its historical context
  3. 15 min Mpatapo and its Akan cultural meaning
  4. 10 min story and discussion: where do they meet and differ?
  5. 20 min accessible, noncompetitive movement with options to rest
  6. 10 min gentle natural breathing; no forced holds
  7. 5 min quiet reflection or optional sound bowls
  8. 5 min takeaway and home practice

Ask: “What is the difference between peace and silence?” “Can boundaries and reconciliation coexist?” “What is one action that could reduce harm this week?”

5 · Your reflection journal

What did you learn from Yama? From Mpatapo? Where do they overlap, and where do they differ? What action will you practice this week?

Instructor notes and cultural care

Introduce this as a thematic comparison, not proof of shared origins. Verify the Mpatapo artwork and interpretations with an Akan cultural source or educator before public distribution. The knot story is original. Avoid pressuring participants to disclose trauma, forgive, or reconcile with unsafe people.

Suggested primary study: Patañjali, Yoga Sutra 2.30–2.31; Akan Adinkra cultural references on Mpatapo. Additional sourcing and community review pending.
