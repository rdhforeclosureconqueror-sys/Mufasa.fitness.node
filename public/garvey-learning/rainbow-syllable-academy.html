1. Learn the Rule Before the Quiz

These are rules for specific syllables, not labels that apply to every sight word. Some words use a different spelling pattern.

🚪

OPEN SYLLABLE

The syllable ends with a vowel. There is nothing after the vowel to close it.

The vowel usually says its long sound — its letter name.

gohimeweno
🔒

CLOSED SYLLABLE

A consonant comes after the vowel. Imagine that consonant shutting the door.

The vowel usually makes its short sound.

jobjetcabwinbus
✨

CLOSED-SYLLABLE EXCEPTION

It looks closed, but the vowel does not make the short sound we expect.

The vowel stays long.

findkindcoldboltmost
🌈 Nia's memory sentence: “If nothing closes my vowel, I'm OPEN and the vowel can say its name. If a consonant shuts the door, I'm CLOSED and usually short. If I look closed but still sound long, I'm a MAGIC EXCEPTION.”
🌟 One lesson at a time: We practice all sight words, but only test a vowel pattern when it clearly fits. Silent-e, vowel teams, and r-controlled sounds will get their own lessons later.

3. Syllable Detective 🕵🏾‍♀️

Identify the syllable pattern only when it truly fits. Only words with a clear Open, Closed, or Exception lesson appear in this challenge.

Is the word Open, Closed, or an Exception?
Teacher tip: An open syllable ends in a vowel (he). A closed syllable ends in a consonant, typically with a short vowel (sit). A closed-syllable exception has a long vowel despite its closed form (find, cold). Other spellings are still practiced in the flashcards and word games, but are not tested as one of these three patterns.
