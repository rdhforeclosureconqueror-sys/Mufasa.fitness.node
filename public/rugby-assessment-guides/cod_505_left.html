braking cod · Measured output

505 turn — left

Time through a planned 180° turn: 5 m from the timing gate to the turn line and 5 m back, after an untimed 10 m run-in.

Why it matters in rugby

Braking, turning and accelerating again help players adjust a chase, recover position and redirect movement.

What this cannot tell you

This version records the left plant leg. The direction is known, so it does not test reading an opponent. Modified 505 tests and hand timing are not equivalent to this timed layout.

How to perform and record it

Use the conventional 505 layout: start 10 m behind the timing line, accelerate through it, turn at the line 5 m beyond it using the designated left plant leg, and return through timing. Record plant leg explicitly, fixed timing and trial rules.

Setup and valid-result checklist
  • Foot reaches turn line; designated plant leg; record layout and invalidate wrong-side turns; modified 505 requires another definition.

What is a good score?

A useful score is a valid measurement that helps answer your development question. Compare your own baseline under the same procedure, then use matched research cohorts as context.

A published average describes that study’s players. It is not a qualifying score, a percentile or a prediction of selection. Match the population, method, surface and trial rule before comparing. Independent studies do not create a precise club-to-international ladder.

Forwards

traditional 505 · mean ± standard deviation

n = 13 · Lazio Rugby TOP12 roster; 27 elite players, 13 forwards and 14 backs; in-season

Method and comparison limits

10 m run-in; gate 5 m before 180° turn; gate height 1 m; best of three; synthetic surface.

Published score is not split by plant leg. It cannot serve as a separate left/right standard or a national-team cutoff.

Ways to develop this quality

These are coaching options, not an automatically assigned workout. Your readiness, experience, assessment findings and team workload guide selection.

Coached deceleration and 180° turns

Practise controlling incoming speed and placing the turning foot before increasing approach speed.

Split-stance strength and controlled lowering

Develop the ability to accept and redirect force where the coach identifies a capacity need.

Reactive tracking after technique

Add a real decision separately, once controlled turning is established.
