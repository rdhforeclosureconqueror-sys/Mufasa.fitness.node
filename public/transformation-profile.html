Your Return Agreement

Everybody gets distracted, tired, discouraged, or off course sometimes. The goal is not perfection. The practice is learning how to return. What do we mean by “return”?

The Art of Returning

Watch a child learning to walk. They fall, rise, wobble, fall again, and rise again. They do not decide that falling means they were never meant to walk. Somewhere inside them is an expectation: I am supposed to be walking. So they return to the attempt.

Your journey deserves that same belief. A missed workout, an imperfect meal, a difficult week, exhaustion, distraction, or a loss of motivation does not erase where you are going. The interruption is not the end of the journey. What matters is your return.

Returning does not mean punishing yourself for lost time. You do not have to make up every missed workout or come back at full intensity. Sometimes the wisest return is smaller: ten minutes instead of an hour, two sets instead of three, a walk instead of a hard session. Maybe you needed rest. Maybe the load was too heavy. Maybe your plan needs to change. The smallest intentional step back toward yourself still counts.

This is also how attention works. Distractions will come. The problem is not that your attention wandered; the skill is noticing where it went and gently bringing it back. We practice the same skill with our bodies, habits, goals, and promises to ourselves.

Do not turn one action into an identity. “I procrastinated” is different from “I am a procrastinator.” “I missed a workout” is different from “I always quit.” Negative self-talk can keep you away longer than the original distraction. Notice what happened, learn from it, adjust what needs adjusting, and return.

At the beginning, excitement may carry you. Eventually there will be a morning when the excitement is gone. That is where your Return Agreement becomes important. It is your plan for the days when you are tired, busy, discouraged, unmotivated, or simply human. You decide in advance how you will find your way back.

Fall. Notice. Learn. Adjust. Return. You are not starting over. You are continuing.

Your return is not a promise to Pocket PT or to your trainer. It is a promise to yourself: when life pulls me away from what matters to me, I will find my way back.
