## Story

Kofi arrived at the first bridge carrying a sack that seemed heavier than the food and tools he had packed. The elder at the river looked at him and asked, “What else have you brought?”

“Nothing,” Kofi answered.

But when another traveler brushed his shoulder, Kofi shouted before the man could apologize. The elder nodded toward the sack. “Then why does your anger weigh so much?”

At the first bridge, Kofi remembered a quarrel he had rehearsed for months. He had won the argument a hundred times in his imagination, yet never found peace. He did not decide the hurt was imaginary. He decided he no longer needed to carry the argument into every new conversation. He set down the first stone.

At the second bridge, a merchant asked whether Kofi had finished the work he had promised. Kofi almost said yes. Instead, he told the truth: he had begun, but he was not finished. The honesty embarrassed him for a moment. The lie would have followed him for days. He set down the second stone.

At the third bridge, Kofi saw a younger traveler solve a problem he could not. Pride urged him to dismiss the answer. Curiosity invited him to learn. He thanked the traveler and felt the third stone fall.

The fourth bridge crossed a crowded market. Kofi noticed how often he changed his words to earn approval. He listened to the people around him, then made a choice that belonged to him. At the fifth, he met someone whose success stirred envy. He practiced celebrating another person's harvest without treating it as proof of his own failure.

At the sixth bridge, rain washed away the path Kofi had planned. He tried to command the weather, the water, and the people helping him. None obeyed. At last he asked, “What can we do with what is here?” Together they found another crossing.

The seventh bridge was narrow and unfinished. Kofi stopped at its entrance. He wanted a perfect crossing, a perfect answer, a perfect version of himself before he moved. But the elder's voice returned to him: “Patience is not standing still forever. It is knowing how to move without being ruled by fear.”

Kofi stepped carefully onto the bridge. He did not become a man who never felt anger, pride, envy, or uncertainty. He became a man who could notice what he carried and choose what to put down.

On the far bank he opened his sack. His tools and food were still there. The stones were gone. For the first time, he understood that self-mastery was not the power to control every feeling. It was the freedom to decide which feelings would guide his next action.
