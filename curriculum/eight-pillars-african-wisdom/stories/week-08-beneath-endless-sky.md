## Story

In the year 2186, Nkabom was a city of light. Its towers glowed after sunset, its streets guided travelers home, and its computers could answer questions before most people finished asking them.

Then, one evening, the lights went out.

Marleigh reached for her tablet. Nothing. Nia pressed her face to the window and gasped.

“Look.”

Above the city, freed from its usual glow, the sky was filled with stars.

Grandmother Abena came to stand beside them. For a while, none of them spoke.

Marleigh finally asked, “How can something so far away make us feel so close to it?”

Abena smiled. “You can explain the light's journey with science. But you may still have questions about what it means to be here, looking up.”

Nia pointed to a bright cluster. “Grandpa used to show us that one.”

“He did,” Abena said. “He wanted you to know that the world is larger than the worries of any one evening.”

They went onto the rooftop. With the city's machines silent, Marleigh noticed sounds she usually ignored: wind against a railing, a distant voice, the quiet breathing of her sister.

Abena carried a small cloth bearing the Gye Nyame symbol.

“What does it mean?” Nia asked.

“In Akan spiritual thought, Gye Nyame means ‘Except God.’ It speaks to the supremacy of God,” Abena said. “It is not a claim that we can explain every mystery. It is a reminder that human knowledge has limits.”

Marleigh thought of the water crisis they had solved by retrieving an old map. Technology had helped, but only after they listened to people whose knowledge the city had forgotten.

“So faith means we stop asking questions?” she asked.

“Not to me,” Abena replied. “Wonder can make a person more curious, not less. And humility can help us use what we learn responsibly.”

Nia asked where the stars went during the day. Marleigh explained that sunlight made them difficult to see. Then Nia asked whether Grandpa's lessons were gone because they could no longer hear his voice.

Abena took her hand. “Some things continue through what we remember and how we live.”

As the night deepened, technicians restored power to the distant districts. Lights returned one neighborhood at a time. Marleigh felt grateful for the people who kept the city working. She also felt grateful for the darkness that had let her see beyond it.

When the power reached their building, Nia asked them to leave the rooftop lights off a little longer.

They sat beneath the endless sky, not because they had solved its mysteries, but because they had learned to be present with them.

The eight-week journey had begun with choices of character and had passed through community, memory, resilience, compassion, courage, and shared prosperity. It ended not with a person who knew everything, but with a family willing to keep learning.

Marleigh looked at Nia and Abena. The future was still waiting. So was the wisdom they would need to meet it.
