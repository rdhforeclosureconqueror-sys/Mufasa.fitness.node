# Pocket PT Rugby Coaching OS — Coach Operations Guide

## What this guide is for

Pocket PT is not meant to make a coach guess what a percentage means or where to type information. The operating rule is:

**Coach records evidence. Pocket PT derives status. Safety restrictions override performance.**

A coach should normally enter facts, observations, test results, video, attendance, availability, and decisions. The system should calculate completion/readiness visuals from those records rather than asking a coach to invent a score.

## The four record types

1. **Coach-entered facts** — name, position, rugby age, attendance, test result, session note, match role.
2. **Coach-observed evidence** — what the player demonstrated, where, under what pressure, how independently, and supporting video/note.
3. **System-derived status** — readiness percentage, competency completion, next prerequisite, profile completion, team dashboard totals.
4. **Restricted status** — medical and safeguarding information. These are permission-controlled and cannot be overridden by a performance score.

## First-time team setup

1. Open **Team Roster**.
2. Select **Add player**.
3. Enter identity, primary/secondary position, development stage/rugby age, emergency/profile details appropriate to your permissions, and initial availability.
4. Save the player. The player now exists even if the profile is incomplete.
5. Open **Player Development** and establish the first development priorities.
6. Baseline **Movement**, **Testing & Conditioning**, **Technical Skills**, and relevant position competencies.
7. Use the first two to four weeks to create evidence before treating any readiness domain as verified.

Do not block coaching because a non-safety profile field is missing. Keep the gap visible until it is completed.

## How competency is gauged

A competency is not "I think this player is 82%." It is a defined behavior with prerequisites and evidence.

Use the following evidence ladder:

| Level | Meaning | Typical evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Not assessed | No usable evidence yet | No observation or baseline |
| Introduced | Player has been taught the skill | Walkthrough / explanation |
| Assisted | Can perform with prompts | Controlled drill with coaching cues |
| Independent — controlled | Performs without prompts in a controlled drill | Multiple clean repetitions |
| Independent — live | Performs at realistic speed/opposition | Live training / scrimmage |
| Repeatable under pressure | Maintains quality under fatigue, speed, choice or opposition | Repeated live evidence |
| Verified / current | Required pathway evidence is complete and still current | Coach acknowledgment + required evidence |

For stage-based skills such as Scrum Ready, the **stage count is the score**. If 6 of 10 required stages are verified, show 60% pathway completion while the actual status remains **Stage 6 verified / Stage 7 next**.

For non-stage assessments, a percentage can summarize multiple criteria, but the criteria must remain visible. One serious safety failure must not be averaged away by strong scores elsewhere.

## What counts as evidence

Every evidence record should answer six questions:

1. **What** competency was observed?
2. **Who** observed it?
3. **Where** was it observed — assessment, training, scrimmage, match?
4. **At what pressure** — technical, controlled, live, game-speed, match?
5. **How independently** — prompts required or independent?
6. **What happened** — a factual note, measurement, or tagged video?

Strong evidence example:

> Tackle — controlled live. Three consecutive repetitions. Head remained to the side/behind, shoulder contact below target line, complete wrap, feet continued after contact. No coach prompt on reps 2–3. Video clips attached.

Weak evidence example:

> Looked good today.

Pocket PT should keep the first type of evidence and discourage the second.

## Player Development

This is the individual athlete's operating record. Coaches use it to answer:

- Where is this player now?
- What is stopping the next progression?
- What is the next best action?
- What evidence supports the current status?

Editable coach information should include position, secondary position, development goals, next actions, selection status, and appropriate profile information. Derived information such as readiness completion and competency totals should update from evidence elsewhere.

## Rugby Readiness

Readiness domains remain independent:

- **Movement Ready** — foundational movement quality/capacity required for the player's current rugby demands.
- **Conditioning Ready** — current conditioning is appropriate for the intended training/match demand.
- **Contact Ready** — prerequisite body position, confidence, neck/shoulder/trunk preparation, and controlled contact behaviors are demonstrated.
- **Tackle Ready** — tackle progression evidence is complete to the required level.
- **Ruck Ready** — safe arrival, gate entry, body position, bind/drive and role behavior are demonstrated.
- **Maul Ready** — safe body position, binding, movement and role behaviors are demonstrated.
- **Lineout Ready** — role-specific throw/jump/support competencies are current.
- **Scrum Ready** — stage-based scrum pathway is verified for the player's role; official competition/front-row requirements remain separate.

### How a coach updates readiness

The coach should **not manually type "Movement Ready = 83"**.

Instead:

1. Open the player's domain.
2. Complete or review its defined assessment/pathway.
3. Log the evidence.
4. Mark the relevant criterion/stage as demonstrated, developing, or not yet demonstrated.
5. Add video or measurements where useful.
6. Pocket PT recalculates the visual completion and next prerequisite.
7. A qualified/authorized person records any medical clearance that is required; coaches cannot manufacture clearance.

## Competency Graph

The graph explains dependencies. Each node should have:

- competency name;
- prerequisites;
- current state: Locked / In progress / Verified / Reassessment due;
- evidence requirement;
- evidence history;
- coach acknowledgment;
- next unlocked node.

To edit a competency, open the node and **log evidence**. Do not drag a locked node directly to Verified without satisfying its prerequisite unless the pathway specifically allows an authorized baseline exemption.

## Movement Intelligence

Movement assessments are observations, not medical diagnoses. Record measurable/visible items such as range, balance, symmetry and movement compensations. Camera/pose estimation can support landmark and angle measurement, but a coach or qualified professional remains responsible for interpretation appropriate to their role.

A movement result should create a corrective pathway and a reassessment date rather than simply a red score.

## Testing & Conditioning

Enter actual test data with units and conditions: sprint time, distance, estimated/actual strength test, jump, conditioning result, body weight where appropriate, date, testing method, and tester. The system should preserve historical tests so the coach sees trend rather than overwriting the previous value.

## Technical Skills

Open the skill pathway, select the player's current stage, and log evidence against the defined coaching factors. Technical skill should progress from shape/control to moving opposition to live/game evidence. Unsafe tackle/contact behavior is a technical failure and triggers regression regardless of other strong criteria.

## Rugby IQ & Tactics

Grade the **decision** separately from the **execution**. A player may choose the correct pass and execute it poorly, or make a beautiful pass when the better decision was to carry. Log both so the development plan is accurate.

## Video Review

Upload/tag a clip to one competency or decision objective whenever possible. Add date, context, pressure level, player, competency, and factual annotation. Video should become evidence attached to the player's history—not a disconnected highlight library.

## Training Planner

A training session is made of editable blocks. Each block should contain:

- start time / duration;
- title;
- group;
- intensity/contact load;
- coaching intent;
- competencies to observe;
- assigned coach;
- equipment/space if needed.

### Add a session

Use **New session**, set date/time/venue/goal, complete the safety/EAP checks, then add blocks.

### Edit a block

Use the menu on the block. Change duration, group, load, intent or observation targets. The total session time should recalculate automatically.

### Remove a block

Choose Remove. Pocket PT should ask for confirmation if evidence assignments or players are attached to that block.

### Player groups

Players should be added to or removed from a contact group based on current readiness and restrictions. A coach cannot place a medically restricted player into a prohibited contact group.

## Match & Selection

Selection is **coach-owned and evidence-informed**.

To change a player's match status, select the player and choose Starter / Bench / Competing / Developing / Not selected as appropriate. To assign a shirt, choose an eligible player for that position. Medical, concussion, safeguarding/event, and competition-specific hard restrictions override the selection action.

The selection evidence percentage is not a safety clearance and should never auto-select the XV.

## Player Welfare

Update welfare from the player's welfare record, not from an ordinary coaching note. Typical coach-facing actions include:

- Game eligible / Limited / Medical hold;
- record possible injury;
- record possible head injury and remove;
- attach provider documentation through the approved secure workflow;
- follow the documented return pathway.

Pocket PT records and enforces restrictions. It does not diagnose and the coach does not provide medical clearance outside their qualification.

## Safeguarding

Safeguarding is a protected workspace. The operating sequence is:

**Listen → Do not investigate → Record facts → Report/route.**

Use **Report a concern** for something seen, heard, disclosed or otherwise received. Record exact words where relevant, immediate danger, source and factual context. The designated safeguarding officer manages the case routing. Possible abuse/crime is referred through the applicable external/statutory process. Normal coaches do not browse confidential case narratives.

## Command Center

The Command Center should be a **derived dashboard**, not a second place to manually maintain the same information.

It should update automatically from:

- roster changes;
- player profile gaps;
- readiness evidence;
- session plan;
- selection state;
- welfare restrictions;
- authorized safeguarding task status;
- coach credential status.

If you add/remove a player in the Roster, the Command Center totals should change automatically. If a player's availability changes to Medical hold, selection and training availability should update automatically. Coaches should never have to edit the same fact on three screens.

## Coach Ready

Coach Ready tracks credentials and internal development separately. Official certificates are uploaded/verified; Pocket PT does not issue World Rugby/USA Rugby accreditation. Team administrators can configure which credentials are required for each coaching role and season.

## What coaches can edit vs what the system calculates

| Item | Coach enters/changes? | System calculates? |
|---|---:|---:|
| Player name / position | Yes | No |
| Attendance | Yes / attendance workflow | Summary/trend |
| Test result | Yes | Trend / comparison |
| Factual competency evidence | Yes | Completion/status |
| Readiness percentage | No, normally | Yes |
| Next prerequisite | Coach can approve/adjust | Suggested from graph |
| Selection decision | Yes | Evidence summary only |
| Medical diagnosis/clearance | No unless qualified/authorized | Never invents |
| Safeguarding finding/guilt | No | Never |
| Safeguarding report | Yes, within permissions | Routing/reminders only |
| Command Center totals | No | Yes |

## Daily coach workflow

**Before training:** review Command Center → welfare restrictions → roster/attendance → training plan → EAP/safeguarding controls.

**During training:** coach the session → log meaningful competency evidence → tag useful video → immediately act on injury/safeguarding concerns.

**After training:** close attendance → add observations → update next actions → review players who changed status.

**Before selection:** check hard restrictions first → role competency → current evidence → coach judgment → publish the decision and next action.

## Data rule for the production build

Real medical and safeguarding data must not be stored in this standalone demo. Production requires authentication, role-based access, encryption, audit logging, secure evidence/document storage, and legal/privacy/safeguarding review.

# Backs Rugby Ready — Coach Operating Guide

Backs Rugby Ready is the backline equivalent of the forward-pack competency pathways. It is used for players in positions 10, 12, 13, 11/14 and 15. It does **not** replace Movement Ready, Conditioning Ready, Tackle Ready, medical status or general Rugby Readiness. It adds the space/decision/role layer that backs must prove.

## What the coach is judging

The Backline Skill/IQ Rating uses six categories: Defensive IQ (20%), Tackling in Space (15%), Attack IQ (20%), Ball Skills (15%), Movement & Evasion (15%), and Role-Specific Skill (15%). The score is 1–99, but it must be supported by evidence. A coach should not raise a rating because the player "looks talented."

Use the rating bands as an internal coaching language: 90–99 impact starter/elite in the program; 80–89 clear starter-ready; 70–79 match-ready/rotation; 60–69 developmental; below 60 not yet ready for that backline role.

**Hard gate:** Tackling in Space below 70 prevents starter eligibility, regardless of speed or total Skill/IQ rating. Medical, concussion, safeguarding and other participation restrictions remain separate hard restrictions and are never averaged into a score.

## Defensive IQ

Observe alignment, numbering up, spacing, inside-shoulder protection, drift-versus-press decisions, edge defense, backfield coverage, reload/fold and communication. The coach should record the decision and the context, not only the result. Example: "13 shot early with 2v2 outside; disconnected wing and created edge break."

## Tackling in Space

Grade tracking angle, footwork before contact, safe head placement, shoulder connection, wrap, finish and recovery/reload. This pathway should progress from controlled tracking to live space and then match evidence. Unsafe head position, illegal contact or repeated arm-only tackling is a technical failure requiring regression/correction, not merely a few points deducted from an average.

## Attack without the ball

Grade depth, width, timing, running lines, decoy quality, support lines, reload and communication. Players should understand that they are being evaluated when they do not receive the ball.

## Attack with the ball

Grade catch quality, passing both directions, ability to commit/fix a defender, pass/carry/kick/reset decisions, evasion, contact choice, ball presentation and continuity.

## Position overlays

Use the universal backs card first, then the player’s position overlay.

- 10 / Fly-half: game management, passing range, tactical kicking, defensive bravery, backline communication.
- 12 / Inside centre: hard-line threat, second-playmaker skill, defensive control, contact skill, decisions under pressure.
- 13 / Outside centre: defensive reads, edge connection, outside break, wing distribution, transition speed.
- 11/14 / Wing: finishing, touchline awareness, high ball, kick chase, last-pass support.
- 15 / Fullback: backfield organization, high-ball security, counterattack decisions, last-line tackling, communication.

## Evidence standard

Each meaningful score change should point to an evidence record containing player, position evaluated, category, competency, score, evidence type (drill/scrimmage/match/video), coach note, optional clip/timestamp, evaluator, date and confidence.

Preferred progression: Drill proves execution → Scrimmage proves decision + execution → Match/Video proves repeatability under genuine pressure.

Example: Player Marcus · Position 13 · Defensive IQ · Edge decision-making · 64 · Scrimmage · "Shoots too early when the wing has an outside threat; hold connection longer." · Next action: 13/wing drift drill and reassess.

## Backline Athletic Index

Keep fitness separate from Skill/IQ. Suggested weights for backs: Speed 25%, Agility 25%, Repeat Effort 20%, Endurance 15%, Power 10%, Strength 5%. Tests can include 10m/20m acceleration, 40m speed, 5-10-5/T-test, Bronco/beep, jumps/medicine-ball throws, and a rugby-specific get-up/reload repeat sprint.

The Athletic Index can support selection evidence, but it cannot override unsafe tackling, poor role understanding, medical restrictions or other hard gates.

# Adult Activate Engine

Use **Activate-Aligned Adult Foundation** for adult rugby-preparation sessions and match-day preparation. The coach selects the current level, delivers the four session parts, watches the Activate 8 coaching cues, and records athlete quality rather than manually editing a readiness percentage.

## What the coach edits
- Attendance status
- Control 1–5
- Balance 1–5
- Technique 1–5
- Trunk stability
- Lower-limb alignment
- Landing quality
- Change-of-direction quality
- Neck control
- Fatigue breakdown
- Pain / injury / concussion concerns
- Factual coach notes
- Coach approval

## What Pocket PT derives
- Hold / regress / progress recommendation
- Team progression recommendation
- Readiness visualization
- Beast Mode candidate eligibility

The coach should never override an active medical or concussion restriction by raising a performance score.

**Foundation:** Activate-Aligned Adult Foundation.  
**Extension:** Pocket PT Beast Mode Rugby Performance System.
