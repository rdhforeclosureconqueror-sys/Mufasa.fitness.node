Coach Operations Manual

Know what it means.
Know what to record.
Know what changes it.

This guide explains how a coach should operate every major area of Pocket PT—from adding a player to proving competencies, building sessions, publishing selection, updating welfare and handling safeguarding concerns.

Operating rule: Coaches record facts and evidence. Pocket PT derives status. Safety restrictions override performance.
01 · Mental model

Not every number is something a coach types.

The system has three kinds of information. Understanding this prevents bad data and makes the dashboard trustworthy.

COACH ENTERED

Facts & observations

Name, position, test result, attendance, selection decision, factual note, tagged video and what happened in training.

SYSTEM CALCULATED

Status & completion

Profile completion, pathway completion, readiness visualization, next prerequisite, trend and Command Center totals.

RESTRICTED

Medical & safeguarding

Permission-controlled data. A performance score cannot override a medical restriction or replace a safeguarding process.

02 · Roster

How to put a player into the system.

The roster creates the player once. Every other module should reference that same record.

1Team RosterSelect Add player. Do not create separate player records in readiness, sessions or selection.
2Identity & roleAdd name, primary/secondary position, unit, rugby age/development stage and basic profile information.
3Initial statusSet current participation/availability only from information you are authorized to record. Leave unknown fields incomplete.
4BaselineOpen the player's Development record and begin Movement, Testing, Skills and position-specific competency baselines.
Removing a player should normally mean “archive,” not erase.

Historical evidence, injury/welfare records and coaching history should remain preserved. Archive the player from the active roster and allow authorized restoration later.

03 · Competency

How do we gauge whether a player actually has it?

Do not ask a coach to invent “82%.” Grade the level of independence and pressure, then let the system calculate the visual completion.

LevelWhat it meansWhat you should see
Not assessedNo usable evidence.No baseline or observation yet.
IntroducedPlayer has been taught the behavior.Can describe/attempt it but is not yet reliable.
AssistedPerforms with coaching prompts.Controlled drill; coach still supplies cues/corrections.
Independent — controlledPerforms without prompts in a predictable drill.Multiple clean repetitions with correct key factors.
Independent — livePerforms with realistic speed/opposition.Live training or scrimmage evidence.
Repeatable under pressureMaintains quality under fatigue, speed, choice or opposition.Repeated successful evidence, not one lucky rep.
Verified / currentRequired pathway evidence is complete and current.Coach acknowledgment plus the required supporting evidence.

✓ Do

Say “Stage 6 verified, Stage 7 is next” or “independent in controlled contact, live evidence still required.”

× Don't

Say “he feels like an 85” and type 85 into readiness with no rubric or evidence.

04 · Evidence

What does “Log Evidence” mean?

Evidence is the reason a status exists. Every important competency should be explainable later.

Every record answers six questions

  1. What competency?
  2. Who observed it?
  3. Where — assessment, training, scrimmage, match?
  4. Pressure — technical, controlled, live, match?
  5. Independence — prompts or independent?
  6. What happened — factual note/measurement/video?

Good evidence example

“Tackle — controlled live. Three consecutive reps. Head stayed to the side/behind, complete wrap, feet continued after contact. No coach prompt on reps 2–3. Video attached.”

Weak evidence

“Looked good today.”

05 · Player Development

The player's home record.

This screen answers: where are they, what blocks progress, what is next, and what evidence supports that status?

EDIT

Player identity

Position, secondary position, development goals, rugby age/stage, coach-defined next actions and selection status.

DERIVED

Readiness cards

These should update when evidence/pathways change. Do not directly type the percentage.

ADD

Evidence history

Use Log observation or Add evidence. Never overwrite old evidence; append a new dated record.

06 · Rugby Readiness

Each domain answers a different safety/development question.

A player can be ready in one domain and still developing or restricted in another.

Movement Ready

Foundational movement quality/capacity appropriate to current rugby demands. Update by completing defined movement assessments and logging evidence.

Conditioning Ready

Current conditioning supports the intended training or match demand. Update from current tests, training exposure and program criteria.

Contact / Tackle Ready

Progress through body position, controlled contact, moving contact and realistic pressure. Unsafe key factors cause regression, not a small score deduction.

Ruck / Maul Ready

Verify safe entry, posture, bind/drive and role behavior through progressively more realistic environments.

Lineout Ready

Role-specific: thrower, jumper and support/lifter competencies are assessed separately.

Scrum Ready

Stage-based pathway. If 6 of 10 required stages are verified, pathway completion is 60%; actual status remains “Stage 6 verified.”

How to update a readiness domain

Open player → choose domain → complete/review its rubric or pathway → log evidence → verify the relevant criterion/stage if authorized → Pocket PT recalculates completion and next prerequisite.

07 · Backs Rugby Ready

The backline equivalent of pack readiness.

Use one universal card for 10–15, then apply the player’s position overlay. Fitness is recorded separately.

COACH ENTERED

Six Skill/IQ categories

Defensive IQ 20%, Tackling in Space 15%, Attack IQ 20%, Ball Skills 15%, Movement & Evasion 15%, Role-Specific Skill 15%.

HARD GATE

Tackling in Space ≥ 70

A high-speed, high-skill player is not starter eligible while the tackle-in-space safety standard is below the program minimum.

SEPARATE INDEX

Backline Athletic Index

Speed 25%, agility 25%, repeat effort 20%, endurance 15%, power 10%, strength 5%. Do not blend this into Skill/IQ.

1Choose player + position10, 12, 13, wing or 15.
2Open categoryRead the exact observable criteria before scoring.
3Log evidenceDrill → scrimmage → match/video. Add note, pressure and confidence.
4Give next actionNo score without a pathway for improvement.

Position overlay

10: game management, passing range, kicking, defensive bravery, communication. 12: hard-line threat, second playmaker, defensive control, contact, pressure decisions. 13: defensive reads, edge connection, outside break, wing distribution, transition. Wing: finishing, touchline awareness, high ball, chase, support. 15: backfield organization, high-ball security, counterattack, last-line tackle, communication.

Player-facing rule

A back is being graded even when they do not touch the ball. Depth, width, timing, running line, decoy work, support, reload and communication are all evidence.

07 · Competency Graph

The graph should explain “why not yet?”

Each node has a prerequisite, evidence requirement, current state and history.

1LockedPrerequisite not met.
2In progressCurrent evidence target.
3VerifiedRequirement demonstrated.
4Reassess dueEvidence is old or role/context changed.

How to edit the graph

Do not manually drag a locked node to Verified. Open the node, inspect prerequisites, log the required evidence, then acknowledge the node. The system unlocks the next node and preserves the previous evidence.

08 · Movement / Testing / Skills / Video

Enter the source data, not the conclusion.

Movement Intelligence

Record visible/measurable movement data and compensations; create corrective work and reassessment. Do not turn movement screens into medical diagnosis.

Testing & Conditioning

Enter the actual result, units, date, method and tester. Preserve historical tests so trend is visible.

Technical Skills

Open the skill pathway, grade key factors and pressure level, and log evidence against the correct stage.

Rugby IQ

Grade decision and execution separately. Correct decision + poor execution is different from wrong decision + beautiful execution.

Video Review

Tag each useful clip to a player, competency, date, environment and pressure level. Video becomes evidence—not a highlight folder.

Correction pathway

When a gap is found, assign a next action/exercise, then reassess. The system should show whether the intervention worked.

09 · Training Planner

Add, edit and remove sessions without losing the reason for them.

1New sessionSet date, time, venue, objective and safety/EAP controls.
2Add blocksDuration, group, load, intent, coach and competencies to observe.
3Edit / reorderUse the block menu to change or move it. Total session time recalculates.
4RemoveConfirm if players/evidence assignments are attached.

Player groups

Add/remove players from Live, Controlled, Technique or non-contact groups based on current evidence and restrictions. A medical hold cannot be overridden simply to fill a drill group.

10 · Match & Selection

The coach owns the shirt. The system owns the audit trail.

Selection evidence informs the decision but never becomes an automatic selector.

Add / change a player

Select a shirt or role → choose an eligible player → set Starter / Bench / Competing / Developing / Not selected → add the reason/next action → publish.

Remove from selection

Move the athlete to Bench/Not selected or unassign the shirt. Keep the selection history rather than deleting the previous decision.

Hard lock example

97% selection evidence + active concussion restriction = NOT ELIGIBLE. Safety is not averaged into a performance score.

11 · Player Welfare

Update participation without pretending to diagnose.

Coach-facing actions

  • Set/record Game eligible, Limited or Medical hold within permissions.
  • Record possible injury.
  • Record possible head injury and remove.
  • Follow the approved return pathway.
  • Attach/record authorized provider clearance through the secure workflow.

Boundary

Pocket PT records restrictions and workflow. It does not diagnose. A coach cannot generate medical clearance outside their qualification or bypass an active restriction.

12 · Safeguarding

Listen → Do not investigate → Record facts → Report.

This is intentionally different from normal player-performance editing.

1ReceiveSomething seen, heard, disclosed or reported.
2Record factsExact words where relevant. Separate fact from assumption.
3RouteSafeguarding officer / approved process; immediate danger gets emergency action.
4Professional processDo not conduct your own abuse investigation.

Who can edit this?

Any permitted person may submit a concern, but case review, restricted narratives and actions belong only to authorized safeguarding roles. Normal coaches should not see confidential case detail just because they coach the athlete.

15 · Operating rhythm

A coach should be able to run the whole day from this sequence.

Before training

Command Center → welfare restrictions → roster/attendance → training plan → EAP/safeguarding controls.

During training

Coach → log meaningful evidence → tag useful video → act immediately on injury/safeguarding concerns.

After training

Close attendance → add observations → update next actions → review anyone whose status changed.

Before selection

Hard restrictions first → role competency → current evidence → coach judgment → publish decision and next action.

Quick glossary

Language every coach should understand.

CompetencyA defined behavior/skill that can be observed and evidenced.
EvidenceThe record explaining why a competency/status exists.
PrerequisiteSomething that must be demonstrated before the next node unlocks.
VerifiedThe required evidence has been acknowledged and is current.
ReadinessDomain-specific participation/development status; not one magic score.
Hard restrictionA safety/medical/regulatory boundary that performance cannot override.
